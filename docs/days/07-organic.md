# Day 7's Blob

Day 7 (Organic) is a petri dish of *Physarum polycephalum*, the yellow slime mold the Paris Zoo calls le blob. Click or tap to drop oats and a simulated slime mold explores the agar, finds the food and links it with tubes, beside a lab notebook that logs what it does. It's set in IBM Plex Mono, with Kalam for the notebook and Permanent Marker for the pink tape label. Nothing is saved, so a reload is a fresh plate.

- **The script is written to be read:** descriptive names, one small function per job (`moveAgents()`, `spreadTrail()`, `addOatScent()`, `growOrStarve()`, called in order by `step()`), a short comment above each block, and every tuning constant at the top with a note on what it does.
- **The mold is Jones's agent model** (2010), run on a 360×360 grid (`GRID` in the frontmatter, `gridSize` in the script). Each bit of mold (an agent) sniffs the slime and the food 9 cells ahead, straight and 0.4 rad to either side (`smell()`), turns toward the strongest, steps one cell and leaves slime behind; the `trailMap` then blurs 3×3 and loses 10% every step. Only one agent may stand in a cell (`occupiedMap`). That rule is what makes a lacy network: without it the agents clump into a few fat tubes or one ring.
- **Pace:** 25 steps a second (`STEPS_PER_SECOND`), at most 3 per frame, so 60 Hz and 120 Hz screens grow alike; a step is one simulated minute in the readout. Slower reads as time-lapse growth; faster looks like swarming. A step costs about 1.8 ms at the resting 8,000 agents in Chrome on my Mac, and the loop pauses while the dish is off screen. Working out the kibble smell at load takes about 60 ms.
- **Population:** it starts as 1,200 agents in a disc and buds from itself up to 8,000 (`RESTING_AGENTS`). Each eaten oat adds 3,000 more (`AGENTS_PER_OAT`), up to 36,000 (24,000 on devices reporting four cores or fewer). With no oats left it starves back toward 8,000, so it blooms when fed and thins out again.
- **Oats** smell through their own `foodMap`, strongest beside the oat and fading out over 28 cells (`OAT_SMELL_RADIUS`). It's kept apart from the `trailMap` so the smell is never drawn: an oat only turns yellow once real mold reaches it. An oat is found once 10 agents step within 10 cells of it in a frame (`NIBBLES_TO_FIND`, `OAT_REACH`), is eaten a little per nibble, then fades out.
- **Oat flakes are drawn to scale,** about 5 mm long in a 90 mm dish (`OAT_LENGTH`, 20 cells). Each one gets its own lumpy outline, narrower at one end, when it's dropped (`makeOatOutline()`), with a creamy middle fading to a pale edge, a few faint broken fibers, sometimes a brown bit of bran at the tip, and a soft shadow; it tints yellow as the mold covers it.
- **Explorers:** every 40th agent ignores the smell and drifts in long, slightly bent paths. They make the hazy fringe around the tubes, and every step can stain the agar a little yellow (`stainMap`), so the dish slowly yellows wherever the mold has been. The pulse along the tubes is shuttle streaming, a brightness wave rippling out from where it started; reduced motion turns it off.
- **Drawing:** the agar canvas is one pixel per cell, colored from a `palette` worked out up front (16 stain levels × 256 slime strengths), and CSS scales it up smoothly. Oats are drawn sharp on a second canvas at screen resolution.
- **The hidden cat** is Dante. He knocked the dish off the counter at 3 A.M. (the notebook's 10/6 entry), and the mold can't grow in a Dante-shaped patch on the left: HiddenCat's outline drawn onto a hidden canvas and copied into the `dishMap` as `CAT_PATCH` (`CAT` in the frontmatter places it, in fractions of the dish). The patch is drawn as plain agar, so a clean plate shows nothing.
  - **Kibble dust** (`kibbleMap`) gives him a faint smell that's strongest at his edge and fades out over 45 cells (`KIBBLE_SMELL`, `KIBBLE_RADIUS`). The mold is drawn to him but can't grow on him, so it wraps a tube around his outline. It's added to the `foodMap`, so it's never drawn either. Unfed, the mold reaches him in about 400–500 steps (16–20 seconds) and has closed a tube around him by about 600 (24 seconds); by about 1,000 (40 seconds) he's crisp.
  - **The hotspot stays hidden until he shows.** `checkForDante()` runs twice a second and unhides the `.cat-spot` once 65% of the agar within 3 cells of him has stained to at least 9 of 15 (`REVEAL_SHARE`, `REVEAL_STAIN`, `RING_WIDTH`). In testing that landed between 500 and 600 steps, as the tube around him closes; it's tuned to err early, since a Dante you can see but can't click feels broken. Until then he can't be clicked. HiddenCat normally shows "Dante was here." on load for anyone who found him before; this page clears that at the reveal, so the label only appears when he's clicked on this visit (the tally in `localStorage` keeps the earlier find). At the reveal the notebook writes "Clean patch, left side. Has… ears?" Fresh plate hides him again.
  - The transparent `HiddenCat` button sits exactly over the patch; finding him adds a line to the notebook.
- **Keyboard:** the dish is a focusable `role="application"`. Arrow keys move a dropper (Shift for small steps), and Enter or Space drops an oat. A hidden status line says where things land by clock position ("at 4 o'clock, near the rim"), and the notebook's live log is a `role="log"`.
